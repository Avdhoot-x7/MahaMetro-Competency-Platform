# Question-Writing Guide for SME Panels
*Maha Metro Competency Assessment – Centre of Excellence (CoE) · v1.3*

A good question tells us **whether a person can do the job**. It should not test whether they can spot the answer from its wording. Every question goes through: **SME draft → peer review → Marathi check → Cell approval → pilot**. After about 20 people have answered it, the platform runs item analysis. Questions with p < 0.30, p > 0.85 or r < 0.20 come back to the panel for rework.

## The 10 rules

1. **Start from a real job situation.** Describe who, where and what has happened. Then ask what the person should do *first*, *next* or *best*. Do not ask for a definition the person could memorise.
2. **Test one point per question.** If two facts are needed, write two questions.
3. **Every wrong option must be a mistake a real employee could make.** Use errors from incident reports, audit observations and trainee answers. If nobody would ever pick an option, delete it.
4. **Write four options for single-answer and situational (SJT) questions, and five or more for multiple-answer questions.** Three good options are better than a fourth silly one. If you only have three good options, tell the Cell.
5. **Keep all options about the same length and style.** The correct answer is often the longest because it carries the qualifications. Give the wrong options the same level of detail.
6. **Do not use "All of the above", "None of the above", "Both A and B", or "वरील सर्व" / "वरीलपैकी काहीही नाही".** Write a real option instead.
7. **Avoid giveaway words.** "Always" and "never" usually mark a wrong option; "may" and "usually" usually mark a right one. Do not repeat a word from the stem only in the correct option.
8. **Avoid negative stems** ("Which is NOT…"). If you must use one, write the word NOT in capitals and use it only once.
9. **Give the source.** Name the SOP, rule-book clause, OEM manual section or circular, and add a one-line explanation of why the answer is right. Mark safety-critical questions for SME validation.
10. **Write in both languages.** The Marathi version must keep the same meaning and difficulty. Keep technical terms (OCC, ATP, MCB) in English, as they are used on the job. Check that the Marathi options are also about the same length.

## Bad → Good examples

**Example 1 – Train operation (D01)**

✗ *Bad:* "What should a train operator do at a red signal?"
(a) Stop the train and follow the rules laid down by the OCC for the situation · (b) Ignore it · (c) Go fast · (d) All of the above
→ The answer is obvious from its length, two options are absurd, and it uses "all of the above".

✓ *Good:* "During a signalling failure your train is held at a stop signal. OCC has not answered the train radio for two minutes and the line ahead looks clear. What should you do?"
(a) Stay stationary and contact OCC by the other means laid down ✔
(b) Move at restricted speed to the next signal, as the line is clear
(c) Ask the Station Controller behind you for permission to proceed
(d) Switch to restricted manual mode, since ATP will limit the speed
→ Each wrong option is a real temptation under pressure, and all options are about the same length.

**Example 2 – Operations planning (D04)**

✗ *Bad:* "Headway means: (a) time between two successive trains ✔ (b) speed of train (c) length of platform (d) number of coaches" — this only tests memory.

✓ *Good (numeric):* "A line's round trip, including turnaround time at both terminals, takes 66 minutes, and 11 trains are in service. What headway can be run (minutes)?" → **6**
→ The person has to apply the idea, and there are no options to guess from.

**Example 3 – Integrity (C04, situational question with graded credit)**

✓ "A contractor's representative leaves an expensive gift at your desk after a bill you processed was cleared. What is the best response?" Options are scored 1.0 / 0.3 / 0.2 / 0.0. Every option sounds reasonable, so the person has to show judgement, not just pick the 'nice' answer.

## Choosing the question type

| Want to test… | Use |
|---|---|
| A decision in an incident | Single answer or situational (SJT, graded credit) |
| Steps in the correct order (isolation, failure handling) | Sequence |
| A calculation (headway, MDBF, GST, TDS) | Numeric with tolerance |
| Recognising a signal, alarm, sound or display | Image / audio / video stem |
| Reading an SOP or circular | Passage with 2–4 linked questions |
| Explaining or communicating | Written or spoken answer (marked by the Cell against a rubric) |

## Before you submit – checklist

☐ Real situation in the stem · ☐ One clear best answer · ☐ Four believable options · ☐ Similar option lengths · ☐ No all/none of the above · ☐ Source and explanation filled · ☐ Marathi complete · ☐ Level (1–5) and role families set

The platform's **Quality checks** box in the question editor warns about rules 4, 5, 6, 9 and 10 as you type. A warning does not block saving, but the reviewer will ask about it.
